Aloo Gobi

Description

Aloo Gobi is a quintessential vegetarian dish that is a staple in Indian households and restaurants worldwide. The name literally translates to "Potatoes" (Aloo) and "Cauliflower" (Gobi). It is a dry curry, meaning the vegetables are sautéed in spices rather than swimming in a heavy gravy.

What makes a great Aloo Gobi is the texture; the cauliflower should be tender and infused with spices like turmeric and ginger, but still retain a slight crunch. It’s a healthy, comforting, and flavorful dish that pairs perfectly with hot rotis or as a side to dal and rice.

Ingredients

  • 1 medium cauliflower, cut into bite-sized florets
  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 2 tbsp oil or ghee
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds (jeera)
  • 1 inch ginger, finely chopped or julienned
  • 2 green chilies, slit lengthwise
  • Spices: 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp coriander powder, and 1/2 tsp red chili powder
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander leaves for garnish
  • A squeeze of lemon juice (optional)

Steps

  1. Prepare the Vegetables: Wash the cauliflower florets and potato cubes thoroughly. Pat them dry to ensure they sauté well rather than steaming.
  2. Temper the Spices: Heat oil in a large pan or kadhai. Add cumin seeds and let them splutter. Add the ginger and green chilies, sautéing for a minute until fragrant.
  3. Sauté Potatoes: Add the potato cubes first, as they take longer to cook. Cover and cook for about 5 minutes on medium heat until they are partially softened.
  4. Add Cauliflower and Spices: Add the cauliflower florets along with turmeric, coriander powder, chili powder, and salt. Toss everything well so the vegetables are evenly coated in the spices.
  5. Slow Cook: Cover the pan with a lid and cook on low-medium heat for 10-15 minutes. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking. The dish is done when the vegetables are fork-tender.
  6. Garnish and Serve: Turn off the heat, sprinkle fresh coriander and lemon juice over the top, and serve hot.

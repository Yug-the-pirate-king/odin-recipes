Chole Bhature

Description

Chole Bhature is a world-famous culinary masterpiece from the heart of North India. It is a soul-satisfying combination of spicy, tangy chickpeas (Chole) and deep-fried, fluffy leavened bread (Bhature). Usually served with a side of pickled onions, green chilies, and a cooling glass of lassi, it is the ultimate comfort food.

The magic lies in the contrast: the soft, pillowy texture of the Bhatura is the perfect vessel for soaking up the rich, dark gravy of the Chole, which is slow-cooked with a special blend of spices including dried pomegranate seeds and amchoor (mango powder).

Ingredients

  • 2 cups chickpeas (kabuli chana), soaked overnight
  • 2 large onions, finely pureed
  • 3 tomatoes, pureed
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tea bags (to give the chole their signature dark color)
  • For the Bhatura dough: 2 cups all-purpose flour (maida), 1/2 cup yogurt, and a pinch of baking soda
  • Whole spices: Cinnamon stick, bay leaf, and black cardamom
  • Powdered spices: Chole masala, turmeric, and red chili powder
  • Oil for deep frying

Steps

  1. Pressure Cook the Chole: Cook the soaked chickpeas with water, salt, and the tea bags until they are soft and tender. Discard the tea bags once done.
  2. Prepare the Masala: Heat oil in a heavy-bottomed pan. Add the whole spices, then sauté the onion puree and ginger-garlic paste until golden brown.
  3. Simmer the Gravy: Add the tomato puree and powdered spices. Cook until the oil separates from the masala, then stir in the cooked chickpeas. Let it simmer for 15 minutes.
  4. Make the Dough: Mix the flour, yogurt, salt, and baking soda. Knead into a soft dough and let it rest for at least 2 hours.
  5. Fry the Bhature: Roll the dough into oval shapes. Heat oil in a deep kadhai and fry the bhature until they puff up like balloons and turn golden brown.
  6. Serve: Plate the hot chole with a dollop of butter on top and serve immediately with the fresh bhature.
